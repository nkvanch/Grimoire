Guide for complete beginners

Never played a tabletop roleplaying game? Start here.

Grimoire is a helper for a game you play with friends around a table, using imagination, a few rules and some dice. This page explains that game in plain words, then shows what the app does for you.

What is a tabletop roleplaying game?

Think of it as a story that a group of friends tells together, where nobody knows the ending.

One person runs the worldThe Dungeon Master (DM) describes where you are, plays every other person and monster you meet, and decides what happens.
Everyone else plays a heroEach player has one character: a stubborn dwarf, a nervous wizard, a kind-hearted thief. You decide what they say and do.
Dice decide the uncertain partsWhen you try something risky, like jumping a chasm or persuading a guard, you roll a die and the rules say whether it worked.
There is no winningThe goal is a good story and a good evening. A session lasts a few hours. A campaign is the same story continued over many sessions.

Most of it happens in conversation: "I check the door for traps." "Roll for it." Some groups use a map and miniatures, many just describe things. Nothing needs a screen.

D&D, "5e" and the free rules

Dungeons & Dragons (D&D) is the best-known game of this kind. Its current version is the fifth edition, which players shorten to 5e. It was first published in 2014 and revised in 2024, and some people call the revision "5.5e".

Part of the rules is published for free, for anyone to use, as the System Reference Document (SRD). Grimoire includes the SRD for both the 2014 and the 2024 rules, so you can make a character and play without buying anything. Grimoire is an independent, free app. It is not made by the company that publishes D&D.

What is on a character sheet?

Your character is described by a handful of numbers. On paper you would write them on a character sheet and keep them up to date by hand. Here is an example, a 3rd-level human fighter:

Example fighterHuman Fighter, level 3
  • Strength16+3
  • Dexterity14+2
  • Constitution14+2
  • Intelligence10+0
  • Wisdom12+1
  • Charisma8−1
  • Armor Class18
  • Hit Points28
  • Proficiency+2
  • Speed30 ft

Each big number is an ability score. The small gold number under it is its modifier, the part you add to dice rolls.

Ability scores
Six numbers for what your character is naturally good at: strength, agility, toughness, knowledge, awareness and force of personality. 10 is average.
Modifier
The bonus that comes from a score. 16 Strength gives +3, so you add 3 when you roll for something strong.
Hit points (HP)
How much harm you can take. At 0 you fall unconscious and start making rolls to stay alive.
Armor Class (AC)
How hard you are to hit. An attacker has to roll this number or higher.
Proficiency bonus
An extra bonus for things your character is trained in. It grows as you level up.

These numbers depend on each other. Change your armour and your AC changes. Level up and your hit points and bonuses change. Keeping all of that right on paper is the part beginners find hardest, and it is exactly the part Grimoire does for you.

How the dice work

Games like this use dice with different numbers of sides, named with a "d": a d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 and d20. "2d6" means roll two six-sided dice and add them.

  1. Roll the d20The twenty-sided die decides most things: attacks, skills and resisting danger.
  2. Add your modifierThe bonus from the matching ability score, plus your proficiency bonus if you are trained in it.
  3. Compare with the targetMeet or beat the target number (a monster's Armor Class, or a difficulty the DM sets) and you succeed.

Advantage means roll the d20 twice and keep the higher result. Disadvantage means keep the lower one. You can roll real dice and type the result into Grimoire, or let the app roll for you.

What Grimoire does for you

Builds your character with youStep by step: species, class, ability scores, background, skills, equipment and spells, in any order. Every number is worked out for you.
Explains every numberTap your Armor Class, a skill or a saving throw and it tells you where the number comes from. A good way to learn the rules as you play.
Keeps track during the gameHit points, spells used, conditions such as "poisoned", rests that recover your strength, and an undo button for mistakes.
Helps whoever runs the gameOver 300 monsters from the free rules, a tool for planning fights, and a turn tracker.

Your first ten minutes

  1. Open the appOn a computer, open it in your browser. On an Android phone, download the app. The install steps explain the warning Android shows.
  2. Choose "Try Sample Character"The first screens offer a ready-made 3rd-level fighter called Aria Stonewall. Nothing you do to her matters, so poke at everything.
  3. Tap a numberTap her Armor Class or a skill and read where it comes from. That is how the game's maths works.
  4. Open the Actions tabEach thing she can do in a fight is a card. Tap one to see what it does and roll it.
  5. Make your ownBack on the Characters tab, create a character. Fighter is a good first class: strong, tough and simple to play.

Finding people to play with

You need a group: a DM and usually three to five players. Ask friends who might be curious, or look for a beginner night at a local game shop or library. If nobody in your group has played before, someone can read the free rules and run a short one-evening adventure (a "one-shot") to try it out. Everyone at the table can use Grimoire on their own phone, and nobody needs an account.

Words you will hear

DM / GM
Dungeon Master or Game Master: the person who runs the game and the world.
PC / NPC
Player character, a hero played by a player. Non-player character, anyone the DM plays.
Session / campaign / one-shot
One evening of play / a story over many sessions / a story finished in one session.
Class
Your character's profession and fighting style: fighter, wizard, rogue, cleric and so on.
Species (race)
What your character is: human, elf, dwarf and others. The 2014 rules call it race, the 2024 rules species.
Level
How experienced your character is, from 1 to 20. You gain levels by adventuring.
DC
Difficulty Class: the number you need to reach on a roll for something to work.
Saving throw
A roll to resist something bad, such as poison or a spell.
Spell slot
A spellcaster's limited fuel for spells. Slots come back after a rest.
Short / long rest
An hour's break, or a night's sleep, that brings back some or all of your strength.
Condition
A temporary state such as poisoned, prone or frightened, with its own effects.
Homebrew
Rules, spells or creatures a group invents for itself. Grimoire lets you build them.

What Grimoire is not

  • It is not a video game. The fun happens between people at the table. The app keeps the numbers right so you can concentrate on the story.
  • It does not replace the DM. The DM still describes the world and makes the calls. Grimoire never decides what happens in the story.
  • It does not teach all the rules. It explains numbers as you go. For the rest, the "Playing the Game" chapter of the free rules is a good read.
  • It is an early version. Free and usable, with rough edges. It runs on Android phones and in a computer's browser, not on iPhone. Tell me what confused you, because that helps most.

Free and private. No price, no account, no ads and no tracking. Your characters stay on your own device. Privacy.
